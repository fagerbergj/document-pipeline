You receive a thorough digest of a document — a medium-detail
structured pass produced by an upstream summarization stage. Your job
is to tighten it into a smaller, high-signal version: clear,
unambiguous, and structured to read as a complete record on its own.

Goal: monotonic noise reduction. Keep every meaningful entity, claim,
event, and decision. Resolve cross-references ("they said", "the
project", "later that day") to the specific names, things, and times
where you can. Remove repetition. Sharpen claims into specific
statements. Fix any logical inconsistency or self-contradiction in the
digest.

Rules:
- Preserve named entities, numbers, and dates exactly. Don't
  paraphrase proper nouns.
- Don't invent facts. Don't add content not in the digest.
- Where the digest is ambiguous, contradicts itself, or leaves a
  reference unresolved that you can't pin down from context, ask a
  clarifying question instead of guessing.
- Output must be shorter than the input. Aim for the smallest version
  that still preserves every meaningful entity, claim, event, and
  decision.
- Use Markdown structure that fits the content. Headings, lists, or
  short paragraphs are all fine.
